Hi! Welcome to my very first "devblog"!
This little page is dedicated to the first project (of hopefully quite a few) that I aim to complete as part of a challenge I set for myself: "Work a single week on a project, record your steps, and see how far you've come. Then publish it on sites, such as itch.io."
To clarify: I didn't want to slave away at my desk for 10h+ each day, which may very well happen during GameJams. Instead I set myself the goal to work around a maximum of 3 to 4 hours a day on the project, which admittedly wasn't much.
The idea for this came to me since I'm someone who probably has approximately a dozen unfinished projects and technical prototypes lying around - some of which I simply never had time or motivation to finish. There a also a few projects from my time at University that I believe have a lot of untapped potential. Ultimately, I just want to wrap up a few projects and if I can make them into games that - at least a few - people enjoy I'm happy.
For this first project I wanted to pick up a neat project I worked on during my Master's degree. While it was a 3-man project with enough credits to amount for a third of an entire semester, I skipped a lot of things we had to submit, such as paper prototypes, design documents and presentations. Furthermore, I mainly focused on gameplay and attempted to innovate on the original idea. So... let's get started.

Day 1: 19th of June, 2023
The concept:
Before beginning development, I of course had to think of a plan, what to do and how to do it. The game would be based around the bullet hell theme and low-poly style of "Project: Equilibrium", a game I had worked on for one of my Computer Science classes. That project had a few neat ideas, such as a constantly changing world state between chaos and order, which would dictate enemy behaviour and player abilities, forcing the player to constantly adapt. While I really liked the concept, I did not want to copy it exactly as that would be lame. Also, the state swapping did have its issues, particularly when only few enemies remained and so you would have to wait a long time before finishing the current wave. This lead me to the decision to put state swapping into the hands of the player, allowing them to manage their ships own energy, similar to Star Trek, etc.
Ship you say? Yes! The original game didn't really have any story, lore or similar beyond, "Hey, you're the icosphere! Go murder cubes and other 'inferior' geometric shapes.". While I didn't want to introduce and deep story, I thought it would be nice to have a proper objective. Something along the lines of: "This is your ship. Survive the enemy onslaught until you can escape.". Yeah, I know, it's not great, but this isn't a game with even a remotely narrative aspect, so who cares ;)

Let's recap & summarize:

  • Visuals: Low-poly, using a lot geometric shapes; Neon-style
  • Enemy spawning: Survivors-like but with waves.

The player should be able to shoot randomly spawning enemies, get some kind of score value, and die if they are hit too many times. Since enemies need some way of spawning I had to spend some time considering how to handle it. In the end I came up with the following system: 2 mathematical functions would describe total enemy amount & maximum enemy tier respectively. Basic enemies, such as 'Runners' would be tier 1, while 'Shooters' and other more advanced enemies would be placed in tiers 2 through 5, which would contain bosses. Depending on this max tier, the available enemy types would be selected and - depending on the total amount of enemies - spawned, based upon their weight. I decided on a total of 20 waves for which I would balance the game. In wave 1 50 enemies of tier 1 should spawn, while in wave 20 1000 enemies of tier 4 or lower should appear. Since tier 5 is reserved for bosses, it is not included in the main function and instead spawned every 10 waves once. Waves are only over once all enemies have been destroyed. To test the weighted spawn script, I assigned 4 enemies of weights 1 000 (a), 100 (b), 10 (c), and 1 (d) to it. The results after 1 million rolls were: 900 154 (a), 89 984 (b), 8 913 (c), and 949 (d), which easily shows that the spawn table is working as intended. As expected, reordering the test enemies arbitrarily yielded similar results.

As you can see, a gameplay baseline was existant on Day 3 and the visual effects gave it at least a small satisfaction value when playing. While still A LOT was missing, the core gameplay loop (except for player death) and most underlying systems were already in place. Day 5 (26.06.23): Energy Redirect, Player Weapons & Enemy Types (foundation) After the 2-day pause I was ready to go again. The plan for today was to start with the energy rerouting feature, meaning the player would be able to swap between different 'stances', each of which had a different effect:
  • Weapons: Faster attack speed, if time allows it, some weapons may receive 'overdrive' features
  • Shield: Higher knockback on contact damage taken, shield blocks the first few damage taken
  • Thrusters: Faster movement speed, shorter dash cooldown
On a sidenote, the player would also receive a tiny invulnerability window when hit, to avoid insane amounts when barely scratching enemies. For player weapons I thought about categorizing everything into a few subcategories, an idea that I had already considered when implementing the test weapons.
  • Projectile weapons: These can shoot any amounts of projectiles in any direction & angle. Their stats vary in attacks per second, spawn positions, projectiles per spawn position, projectile spread (in angles), and, of course, the projectile to shoot, as well as its stats.
  • Laser weapons: These are "hitscan" weapons that may or may not project lasting effects. Their stats include maximum range, aim speed, damage per second, and if they penetrate targets.
  • AoE weapons: These can be any type of weapon that uses specific AoE markers before actually firing. They can range from an orbital strike to artillery rockets/missile barrages and so on...
The great advantage of including AoE weapons was that the marker/area indicators could be reused for enemy attack markers. This would allow me to "easily" add multiple types of enemies. Of course this would take a lot of time, so I planned ahead and decided to just think about enemies today and focus on implementing them tomorrow:
  • Runner: Already in the game
  • Shooter: Shoots a basic projectile at the enemy
  • Bomber: Stands still and then launches a slowly moving artillery rocket at a location close to the player. If the target location was not on the platform (and didn't hit ANYTHING - yeah friendly fire would be neat), the rocket would simply drop down without detonating
  • Laser: Similar to bomber, but with a laser and no friendly fire
  • Bosses: Of course bosses would be low priority, but I thought of 2 that may be fun to fight - this game was intended as a bullet hell after all
I had a lot of other ideas regarding elite enemies (stronger versions of normal mobs), more bosses, different weapon damage types (kinetic, laser, explosion, etc.), but frankly these are some features that I just won't have time to implement. IF I continue working on this project in the future I may, however, decide to add them.
